A recommendation system where the evaluation protocol matters as much as the model.

NewsLens studies how much apparent recommender quality survives once the data is evaluated in the order recommendations could actually have been made. The current v0.3.0 system combines leakage-safe ranking experiments with reproducible serving and failure analysis.

At a glance

  • MIND-small: 156,965 impressions, 51,282 articles
  • 125,572 train / 31,393 validation impressions, chronological
  • NDCG@10 0.3664 · Recall@10 0.5955 · Hit Rate@10 0.6762
  • 927 content-model abstentions recovered by training-only fallback
  • 1,000-replicate paired bootstrap
  • DuckDB + SQL audit/feature queries
  • FastAPI + non-root Docker/Compose + GHCR
  • 400+ automated tests

Leakage-safe evaluation

All fitting happens on the chronological training partition: popularity counts, TF-IDF vocabulary, and engagement features. The split explicitly verifies that the latest training timestamp precedes the earliest validation timestamp.

Ranking and fallback

The content model handles 30,466 validation impressions. The remaining 927 — mostly cold-start users plus zero-signal histories — are routed to a popularity model fitted only on training data. The score types are not blended because cosine similarity and click counts are on incompatible scales.

Measured improvement

The selected content + fallback system reaches NDCG@10 0.3664, Recall@10 0.5955, and Hit Rate@10 0.6762. Paired 95% bootstrap intervals for the gains over content-only exclude zero under the fixed offline protocol.

Failure analysis

The project reports subgroup behavior by history length, article category, and training exposure. One unresolved result is intentionally retained: clicked articles with zero training exposure rank better than the most-exposed band under the current cohort definition. The repo treats that as a confounded observation to investigate, not a causal conclusion.

Serving and reproducibility

Versioned, checksummed model artifacts are loaded by FastAPI. Liveness and readiness are separate, the Docker image runs non-root, CI validates both Python behavior and container readiness, and multi-platform images are published to GHCR.

Current limit / next model step

No online experiment has been run and no neural recommender is claimed yet. A high-value next step is a learned two-tower retrieval / neural ranking stage evaluated under the same chronological protocol, rather than adding complexity without a controlled baseline.
