Georgia Tech ASDL · sponsored project · scientific and applied ML

Connecting lifecycle, economics, and transport models for sustainable aviation fuel.

GREEN TEA is a sponsored Georgia Tech group project that evaluates the technical and economic effects of converting forest residue into sustainable aviation fuel. The sponsor was the U.S. Endowment for Forestry & Communities.

At a glance

  • Forest-residue sustainable aviation fuel
  • Lifecycle and techno-economic modeling
  • GREET surrogate model
  • Economic and transportation model integration
  • Tableau decision dashboard
  • Delivered to sponsor team

The project problem

Converting forest residue to sustainable aviation fuel involves linked technical, environmental, economic, and transportation decisions. GREEN TEA brought those models together so that a user could explore the effects of assumptions across the system rather than viewing each model in isolation.

My contribution

I built a data-driven surrogate of GREET (Greenhouse gases, Regulated Emissions, and Energy use in Technologies), a life-cycle analysis tool developed by Argonne National Laboratory. The surrogate supported faster lifecycle analysis within the broader model workflow.

I also integrated the lifecycle surrogate with the project's economic and transportation models, then helped build a Tableau dashboard for model exploration and decision support.

Delivery

The completed dashboard was handed over to the U.S. Endowment for Forestry & Communities sponsor team for its use. The project is one part of the portfolio's larger applied-ML thread: translate a technical model into a reproducible tool that a non-modeling stakeholder can actually use.
