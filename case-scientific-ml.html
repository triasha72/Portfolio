Learning when a cheap approximation is accurate — and where it is likely to fail.

This project is a from-scratch study of surrogate modeling for expensive engineering and scientific simulations. The current public repository contains three focused notebook experiments covering Gaussian Processes, engineering surrogate modeling, and method comparison.

Branin GP surrogate

Using 20 Latin-hypercube samples, the Gaussian Process reaches R² = 0.9553. More important than the headline score, the uncertainty map correctly grows in regions with weak sample coverage.

Cantilever-beam surrogate

The first 30-sample attempt reaches only R² = 0.64 with high average error. Increasing coverage and log-transforming inputs spanning orders of magnitude improves the final experiment to R² = 1.0 and MAPE = 0.26% with 80 samples. The point is the diagnosis: input representation mattered more than changing the model.

Method comparison

On the Rosenbrock benchmark, the current notebook compares response-surface modeling, Gaussian Processes, and radial-basis functions. The GP performs best under the defined experiment; the RSM fails to capture the nonlinear valley, while the RBF offers a faster but less accurate middle ground.

NURBS-BEM and operator learning

The companion NURBS-BEM project builds a mesh-free boundary-element electromagnetic solver from the integral formulation, generates multifidelity datasets for these surrogate studies, and documents the diagnosis of a frozen operator-learning run.
